## SOURCE 1: CUET Exam Pattern & Key Facts (NTA Official)

### Exam Structure
- **Mode**: Computer Based Test (CBT)
- **Duration**: 60 minutes per section
- **Questions**: 50 per section (attempt 40 for domain subjects)
- **Marking**: +5 correct, −1 incorrect, 0 unattempted
- **Medium**: 13 languages available
- **Sections**: Section IA (Languages), Section IB (Languages), Section II (Domain), Section III (General Test)

### Important Changes in CUET 2025-2026
- Hybrid mode introduced (CBT + Pen-and-Paper in select centers)
- NTA Score normalization across shifts
- General Test now has 60 questions (attempt 50)
- Domain subjects: 50 questions (attempt 40)

## SOURCE 3: Key Formulas & Quick Reference

### Economics Formulas

```
National Income:
  GDP_MP = GDP_FC + Net Indirect Taxes
  NDP = GDP − Depreciation
  NNP_FC = GDP_MP − Depreciation + NFIA − NIT
  National Income = NNP at Factor Cost
  
  Income Method: NI = Compensation of Employees + Operating Surplus + Mixed Income + NFIA
  Expenditure Method: GDP_MP = C + I + G + (X − M)
  
Money & Banking:
  Money Multiplier = 1 / LRR
  Total Credit Creation = Initial Deposit × (1/LRR)
  
Income Determination:
  Y = C + I (2-sector)
  C = C̄ + bY  (where b = MPC)
  Multiplier (K) = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS
  ΔY = K × ΔI
  
Budget Deficits:
  Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts
  Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excl. borrowings)
  Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments

Consumer Theory:
  MRS = MUx/MUy = Px/Py (at equilibrium)
  Cardinal: MUx/Px = MUy/Py = MUm
  
Production:
  TP is maximum when MP = 0
  AP is maximum when MP = AP
  MC < AC → AC is falling
  MC > AC → AC is rising
  MC = AC at minimum AC
  
Elasticity:
  Ed = % change in Qd / % change in P
  Ed = (ΔQ/ΔP) × (P/Q)
  Total Expenditure Method:
    Ed > 1: TE ↑ when P ↓
    Ed = 1: TE unchanged when P changes
    Ed < 1: TE ↓ when P ↓
```

```
Fayol's 14 Principles:
  1. Division of Work      8. Centralisation
  2. Authority & Responsibility  9. Scalar Chain
  3. Discipline            10. Order
  4. Unity of Command      11. Equity
  5. Unity of Direction    12. Stability of Personnel

  Motivators (Satisfaction): Achievement, Recognition, Work itself, Responsibility, Growth

  Product: Branding, Packaging, Labelling, Product Life Cycle

  District Commission: Up to ₹1 crore
  State Commission: ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore
  National Commission: Above ₹10 crore
  Consumer Rights: Safety, Information, Choice, Heard, Redressal, Education
```

## SOURCE 4: English Literature Quick Notes

### Flamingo — Prose Summaries

**The Last Lesson (Alphonse Daudet)**
- Setting: Franco-Prussian War, Alsace region
- M. Hamel's last French lesson before German becomes compulsory
- Theme: Love for mother tongue, patriotism, value of education
- Key quote: "When a people are enslaved... they hold the key to their prison"

**Lost Spring (Anees Jung)**
- Two stories: Saheb (ragpicker from Dhaka) and Mukesh (bangle-maker from Firozabad)
- Theme: Child labor, poverty, lost childhood, broken dreams
- "Seemapuri is a place on the periphery of Delhi yet miles away from it"

**Deep Water (William Douglas)**
- Author's fear of water from childhood near-drowning
- Overcomes phobia through practice and determination
- Theme: Conquering fear, Roosevelt's quote about fear itself

**The Rattrap (Selma Lagerlöf)**
- Metaphor: World as a rattrap with material baits
- Vagabond (former rat trap seller) transforms through kindness
- Theme: Essential goodness in humans, transformative power of love

**Indigo (Louis Fischer)**
- Champaran episode in Gandhi's life
- Sharecroppers exploited by British indigo planters
- Theme: Civil disobedience, self-reliance, justice

**Poets and Pancakes (Asokamitran)**
- Life at Gemini Studios, Madras
- Satire on film industry, literary figures, and MRA
- Theme: Contrast between art and commerce

### Flamingo — Poetry Key Points

**My Mother at Sixty-Six (Kamala Das)**: Pain of aging parent, fear of separation, simile of "pale as a late winter's moon"

**Keeping Quiet (Pablo Neruda)**: Appeal for introspection, silence ≠ inactivity, counting to twelve

**A Thing of Beauty (John Keats)**: "A thing of beauty is a joy forever," nature's healing power

**Aunt Jennifer's Tigers (Adrienne Rich)**: Gender oppression, contrast between art (free tigers) and reality (burdened aunt)

### Vistas — Key Points

**The Third Level (Jack Finney)**: Charley finds third level at Grand Central Station → escape to 1894 Galesburg

**The Tiger King (Kalki)**: Satire on royal power, Maharaja kills 99 tigers but dies from a toy tiger

**The Enemy (Pearl S. Buck)**: Dr. Sadao treats wounded American POW despite wartime Japan, ethical dilemma

**On the Face of It (Susan Hill)**: Derry (scarred face) and Mr. Lamb (tin leg) → accepting differences

**Memories of Childhood (Zitkala-Sa & Bama)**: Discrimination against marginalized communities (Native American & Dalit)

## SOURCE 6: Quantitative Aptitude — Key Shortcuts

```
Percentage Tricks:
  x% increase then x% decrease = (x²/100)% decrease always
  Successive discounts: Net = a + b − ab/100
  
Profit & Loss:
  SP = CP × (100 + Profit%)/100
  CP = SP × 100/(100 + Profit%)
  If marked price and discount: SP = MP × (100 − Discount%)/100
  
Ratio & Proportion:
  If a:b = m:n, and each increased by k: compare (m+k)/(n+k)
  
Time & Work:
  If A does in 'a' days, B in 'b' days: Together = ab/(a+b) days
  
Speed, Time, Distance:
  Speed = Distance/Time
  Relative speed (opposite): Sum of speeds
  Relative speed (same direction): Difference of speeds
  km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18
  
Simple Interest: SI = PNR/100
Compound Interest: A = P(1 + R/100)^N

Average: Sum of observations / Number of observations
  New average when one removed: (Total − removed)/(n − 1)
```

## SOURCE 7: Logical Reasoning — Pattern Guide

### Number Series Patterns
- **Arithmetic**: Constant difference (2, 5, 8, 11...)
- **Geometric**: Constant ratio (3, 6, 12, 24...)
- **Squares**: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25...
- **Cubes**: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125...
- **Fibonacci**: Each = sum of previous two (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8...)
- **Alternating**: Two interleaved series
- **Difference of differences**: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30... (differences: 4, 6, 8, 10...)

### Coding-Decoding Common Patterns
- **+2 shift**: A→C, B→D, C→E...
- **Reverse**: WORD → DROW
- **Number coding**: A=1, B=2... or A=26, B=25...
- **Symbol substitution**: Each letter mapped to specific number

### Syllogism Rules
1. All + All = All
2. All + No = No
3. All + Some = No Conclusion
4. Some + All = Some
5. Some + No = Some Not
6. No + All = Some Not (reverse)
